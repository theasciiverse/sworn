**SWEAR THE OATH. BREAK IT WHEN IT PAYS.**

The year 800. You are a lesser noble with one hold and a handful of spears,
and four peoples share the realm: Saxons, Danes, Bretons and Moravians.
Each people has its crown seat, and the first noble to sit in theirs as
king, answering to no one, wins. Climb from lord to count to duke, and
then reach for the crown.

Every season all orders are secret and take effect at once. Raise troops,
march on your neighbours, take the towns that pay and the fortresses that
hold. Columns that meet head-on along a road fight it out in the field
before either one arrives. Then the season turns and the report tells you
who kept their word. In winter nobody marches; winter is for letters.

And letters can say anything. Only an offer binds: gold, land or an oath of
fealty, carried out before the marching if both sides can still pay.
Promise to stand with an ally and stay home. Buy a year's peace from the
lord on your border. Kneel to the strongest Saxon now and turn on them when
you have grown. March on your own liege and the whole realm hears of it.

- **The crown** needs your people's seat, two fifths of the realm held by
  you and your vassals, a name worth 20 prestige, and every other noble of
  your people sworn to you somewhere down the chain. If no one manages it
  in thirty years, the highest score wins
- **Four peoples:** Saxon troops cost less to keep, Danes hit harder,
  Bretons hold their walls, Moravian towns pay more
- **Computer nobles** see only what you would see, take good deals, write
  to you when your lands meet, ask tribute when they are strong, plead when
  they are not, gang up on whoever comes near the crown, and as your
  vassals, march where your letters send them
- **Three difficulties** against the computer: on Gentle their lands pay
  them a third less than yours, on Hard half again as much
- **Up to eight players** around one table, found on your local network or
  joined across the internet by the host's id. A player who drops is held
  by a steward until they come back; the host sets a timer on the seasons
  and how many years the game runs, ten, twenty or thirty
- **Battles flash on the map**, and at the end come the standings and
  charts of every noble's rise and fall

## Controls

- **Arrow keys** or the mouse: choose a province; **n** the next of yours
- **r**: raise troops; **m**: march (**b** to fight on someone's behalf)
- **w**: write a letter, with an offer if you like; **a**: accept an offer
- **1** to **5**: province, orders, letters, nobles, chronicle
- **e**: end the season; **u**: take your orders back; **?**: all keys

## Options

- `--host`: host a table for others
- `--join`, `--join ID`: find tables on the local network, or join one by its id
- `--seed N`: new games on realm N

## History

In 2017 a small studio called Lurebound announced Warwick, a "multiplayer,
terminal-based strategy game for linux". Lesser nobles in an early medieval
realm would rise by war, bribes and alliances until one of them was
crowned. The beta signup opened. The documentation page said "Coming
soon!" And then nothing came. By 2026 even its repositories had vanished,
and all that was left was a page in the Wayback Machine and a little
command-line client for a server nobody ever saw.

Asciiverse read that promise in October 2026 and decided the game deserved
to exist. Sworn was built new from the ground up that November: a realm of
its own, four peoples, seasons where every order lands at once, and offers
that bind when promises do not. First the computer nobles learned to deal,
then to scheme, then to stop selling peace to whoever was winning. Then
two terminals sat side by side on one machine, one hosting and one
joining, and a letter offering two gold for peace crossed the wire and was
accepted. Somewhere in the fifth year a crown was taken, and neither of
them was wearing it.
